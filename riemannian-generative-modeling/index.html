02 / THE GEOMETRY OF SO(3)

Nine entries. Three degrees of freedom.

A rotation matrix preserves lengths and angles. Its columns form an orthonormal frame, and its determinant is +1, preserving handedness:

The orthogonality condition imposes six independent local constraints on nine entries, leaving three degrees of freedom. Nearby rotations therefore admit three local coordinates. Globally, SO(3) has the topology of a three-dimensional ball whose antipodal boundary points are identified. It has no single Euclidean coordinate chart covering the entire space. [1]

A straight line through matrix space generally violates the rotation constraints. A geodesic instead stays inside SO(3), under the standard angular metric:

A cloud of orientations, not a cloud of positions.

08 / THE CENTRAL QUESTION

How much of a complex probability distribution can a generative model preserve when it has only a few steps to produce samples?

Geometry constrains valid motion. Multimodality requires retaining distinct possibilities. Symmetry makes different orientations observationally indistinguishable. A limited evaluation budget leaves less room to correct approximation errors. Their interaction makes efficient generation on rotation manifolds a compelling mathematical problem.

This page introduces the mathematical ideas motivating my ongoing exploration of efficient generative modeling on rotation manifolds. It is an educational exposition, not a report of unpublished experimental results.
